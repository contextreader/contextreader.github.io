Comparison

Context Reader vs Reverso Context

Reverso Context shows how a word or expression is used across millions of real sentences from bilingual texts, and you pick the translation that fits. Context Reader reads the sentence you are looking at and explains the one meaning that fits it, in your language. If you are learning a language, Reverso does more. If you are stuck on one word in something you need to understand, that is the job Context Reader was built for.

Written by the makers of Context Reader. Every fact about Reverso here was checked on Reverso’s own pages on 21 September 2026 — linked where it appears.

The difference, in one sentence

The culture came back positive after forty-eight hours, so the ward started antibiotics.

A corpus toolgives you the ways culture is translated — the arts, a company’s habits, a lab sample grown to find bacteria — with real example sentences for each, and leaves the choice to you. Reverso puts it this way: “Pick the right translation for your context thanks to real-life sentences.”

Context Readersends the word together with the paragraph around it, so the model makes that choice — here it has the ward and the antibiotics to go on. You get one meaning, in your language, with a short explanation tied to this sentence.

Neither approach is simply better. Seeing many real examples teaches you how a word behaves; being told what it means here lets you keep reading. Which one you want depends on why you are reading.

Where Reverso Context is better

Idioms, expressions and slang

Reverso lists over 100,000 expressions, and its browser extension widens your selection to catch one: select suit in “public speaking is not his strong suit” and it looks up strong suit. Its site covers slang and everyday phrases that dictionaries miss.

Real bilingual examples

Reverso’s examples are real sentences from bilingual texts — film dialogue, official documents, articles — shown in both languages. You see a word used for real, many times over. A Context Reader answer is written by a language model for your sentence: often exactly what you needed, but generated, so it can be wrong in a way a real example cannot.

Learning, not just reading

Reverso keeps a history of what you looked up, lets you star favourites into lists synced across devices, and turns them into flashcards and quizzes with spaced repetition. It has pronunciation in different accents and a visual dictionary of 40,000 images. Context Reader keeps no list; it is built for getting through the page in front of you.

Films, phones and desktops

Reverso’s extension translates Netflix and YouTube subtitles on hover, and Reverso runs as a website, an iOS and Android app, and a Mac and Windows app. Context Reader is a desktop browser extension only: Chrome, Brave and Edge.

Nothing to set up

You install Reverso and use it. Context Reader needs a free Gemini API key from Google first. It takes about a minute and asks for no card, but it is a step Reverso does not have.

Where Context Reader is different

It answers for your sentence

The paragraph around the word goes with every lookup, so the answer is the sense that paragraph uses — the finance bank or the river one, the clinical acute or the geometric one. If you want more, More shows the word in a short scenario set in the same world as the page, General gives its other meanings, and Simple says it again in the plainest words.

It works inside one language

Reading English with English chosen, you get the word in plainer English rather than a translation. That helps with a register you don’t use every day — a medical report, a lease, a paper’s methods section. Reverso has English definitions too; Context Reader’s is the meaning this sentence uses.

More languages, not all equally good

Context Reader answers in 110 languages — the ones Google documents its Gemini models as understanding. Reverso’s extension page lists 28. But only English and Sinhala in Context Reader carry worked examples checked by a speaker; the other 108 rely on what the model already knows, and a specialist term can come back with its everyday meaning. Reverso’s examples, in the languages it has, are real translations.

Free with no paid tier, and no ads

There is nothing to buy. It can be free because there is no server: your lookup goes from your browser to Google’s Gemini API on your own key. Reverso is free with ads; Reverso Premium removes them and adds more results and examples, unlimited saved vocabulary and AI translation, for $9.99 a month or $6.49 a month billed yearly ($77.88), with 75% off for students and teachers.

No account, and the code is open

The extension has no account, analytics or telemetry, and is MIT-licensed, so that can be checked. The trade-off is Google’s: on a free key, Google’s terms let it use what you send to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it. A paid key is not used that way. The privacy policy has the detail.

Which one to use

Reverso Context, if you are…

  • learning a language and want to see how words are really used
  • working with idioms, slang or set expressions
  • building vocabulary you want to review later
  • watching films or reading on your phone

Context Reader, if you are…

  • reading something you need to understand, and one word is in the way
  • reading a field’s own English — medicine, law, research
  • reading in a language Reverso doesn’t cover
  • after something free, open and account-free

Questions

Is Context Reader a free alternative to Reverso Context?

For understanding a word while you read, yes: it is free with no paid tier, and it needs your own free Gemini key. It is not a replacement for Reverso’s example sentences, idiom search or learning tools, which it does not have.

Does Reverso look at the sentence I’m reading?

Its extension reads the words next to your selection to catch multi-word expressions. The translations and examples it then shows come from its corpus rather than being written for your sentence; you choose the one that fits.

Which is better for idioms?

Reverso. Its expression search and real examples are the strongest thing about it.

Can Context Reader be wrong?

Yes. Its answers are generated by a model, and it is less reliable in languages the model saw less of. The EN button shows the same answer in English, which is a quick way to check.
